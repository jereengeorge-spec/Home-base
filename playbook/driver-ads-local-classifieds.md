## 0. ⚠️ Read this before posting anywhere

You asked for Facebook Marketplace and Craigslist. **Both platforms explicitly prohibit this exact use.** Not a gray area — it's in their published rules:

| Platform | The rule | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Facebook Marketplace** | Prohibits **non-physical items**, including services, subscriptions, and digital products. **Job listings are separately prohibited**, specifically to reduce misleading and fraudulent opportunity postings. | Listing removed. Repeat violations → **Marketplace access revoked**, which can extend to the Facebook account. |
| **Craigslist** | Lists **affiliate marketing** among prohibited posts, alongside MLM and get-rich-quick schemes. Also bars posts that are "signposts to other websites." Overposting (more than once per 48h) and miscategorizing are independent flag reasons. US gigs sections are **paid, $3–10 per post**. | Ghosted (posted but invisible to others) or flagged and removed. **You'd be paying $3–10 per post to get ghosted.** |

**A driver-referral post is a non-physical service listing with an affiliate link in it. It violates both, twice over.** Craigslist is the worse of the two — you pay per gig post and affiliate content is named directly in their prohibited list.

So I've written the copy in the classifieds voice you asked for — short, plain, local, no marketing gloss — but **pointed it at the channels where posting it is actually permitted.** The voice is right; the destination changes. Each variant below is labeled with the channels it's cleared for.

### Where this genuinely works

| Channel | Cost | Why it fits | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Local Facebook Groups** — "[City] Gig Workers", "[City] Rideshare Drivers", "[City] Jobs" | Free | ✅ **Your best channel.** People there are actively asking these questions. Groups permit what Marketplace forbids. | Each group has its own admin rules. Read pinned rules; many require permission for promotional posts. **Ask an admin first.** |
| **Reddit** — r/uberdrivers, r/[yourcity], r/beermoney | Free | Genuinely useful answers get upvoted | Most subs ban referral links outright. **Check sidebar rules per sub.** Some have dedicated referral threads — use those. |
| **Nextdoor** | Free | Hyper-local, neighbors looking for supplemental income | Has a "Business" post type — use it, don't disguise a promo as a neighbor post |
| **Your own accounts** | Free | No platform gatekeeper | Reach is whatever you've built |
| **Physical: laundromats, community boards, car washes, community colleges** | ~$0 | Zero platform risk, genuinely local, almost nobody does it | Slow. QR code to your landing page. |

**The economics here are the good news.** All of these are $0 media cost, which puts you in Scenario D from the main playbook — the profitable lane. There's no CPC to clear, so **any** conversion is margin. The constraint is reach and patience, not arithmetic.

## 1. Rules every version below already follows

Baked into the copy — don't edit them out:

- **Affiliate disclosure before the link**, not buried
- **Never implies you're hiring or that you're Uber.** You're not the employer. Stating this plainly is both legally necessary and, usefully, more credible
- **No Uber logos, wordmarks, or app screenshots.** Naming "Uber" in text to identify what you're referring to is permitted; branding is not
- **Zero unsourced earnings claims.** Every number is a `[BRACKET]` you fill from *your own app, your own city, today*
- **Requirements stated up front** — this is the qualification filter, and it's the highest-leverage thing in the whole funnel (see red team, Attack 8)
- **No fabricated testimonials.** If you make a video with an AI presenter, you need an AI disclosure too — text posts you write yourself don't

**Before your first post, fill in:** `[CITY]` · `[$BONUS]` · `[N]` trips · `[DAYS]` window · `[LINK]` · `[VEHICLE YEAR]`

If you can't fill `[$BONUS]` from your own Driver app right now, **you're not ready to post** — that number is the entire offer, and it changes weekly.

## 3. What not to write

The version that gets you removed, and possibly worse:

> ~~🚗💰 **UBER IS HIRING IN [CITY]! $2,175 SIGN-UP BONUS!** 💰🚗~~
> ~~Sarah made $1,800 her first week! No experience needed! Start today!~~
> ~~[uber logo] Apply now: [link]~~

Six problems in four lines:

1. **"IS HIRING"** — you're not the employer, and Uber drivers are contractors, not employees. Misrepresentation.
2. **"$2,175"** — that figure circulating online is the new driver's *earnings guarantee*, a floor on what [N] trips would pay anyway. It's not a bonus and it's not your city's number. Using it is a false-advertising exposure.
3. **"Sarah made $1,800"** — a fabricated testimonial. If Sarah is AI-generated, it's a fake endorsement from a person who doesn't exist.
4. **"Start today"** — false. Background check takes days.
5. **The Uber logo** — barred by the referral rules and a trademark exposure.
6. **No affiliate disclosure** — FTC issue independent of everything else.

Every one of these is also a *conversion* problem: it recruits people who churn before [N] trips, so **you pay nothing and earn nothing.** The honest version isn't the compliant-but-weaker option. It's the one that pays.

## 4. Posting discipline

**Cadence**
- **One post per group per week, maximum.** Two is spam and admins have long memories.
- Rotate variants — same post repeated reads as a bot.
- Stagger across groups; don't blast five in one hour.

**Earn the right to post**
- Join, then answer 5–10 questions with **no link at all** before you ever post a promo. In gig groups the fastest route to a permanent ban is joining and immediately dropping a link.
- Read pinned rules. Many groups require admin approval for promotional posts — **ask.** Admins who say yes will often let you post regularly.

**Respond to every comment.** A single "how long did your background check take?" answered well converts better than ten new posts.

**Track what works.** Use a distinct link per channel — Impact supports sub-IDs — so you know whether the reply came from Facebook, Reddit, or the laundromat QR code. Without that you're optimizing blind.

**Stop signals**
- Post removed by admin → don't repost. Message the admin, ask what's allowed.
- Two removals in one group → stop posting there entirely.
- Any warning from a platform → stop everywhere, re-read rules, then reassess.

## 5. The landing page

Every variant sends to `[LINK]`. That page is required — it's where the affiliate link lives, it's what the Affiliate Program evaluates you on, and it's what makes the post useful rather than spam.

**Title:** *Driving for Uber in [CITY] — Requirements, Timeline, and What You Actually Clear*

Sections, in order:

1. **Affiliate disclosure** — first thing on the page, above everything
2. **Requirements checklist** — vehicle, license, insurance, background check
3. **Real timeline** — application → check → inspection → activation
4. **The bonus, precisely** — `[$BONUS]`, `[N]` trips, `[DAYS]` window, screenshot-dated, with the fine print
5. **Honest earnings** — gross vs. after gas, depreciation, self-employment tax
6. **Who this doesn't work for** — financing a car for this, under 10 hrs/week, gas-hungry vehicles. *Counterintuitive but true: this section converts, because it's proof you're not just chasing the commission.*
7. **The link**, with disclosure repeated next to it
8. **FAQ** from real questions you get in the groups

**Keep it a genuine resource.** That's a Meta requirement if you ever run paid ads, it's what the affiliate program wants to see, and it's the difference between a post people thank you for and one they report.
