The shortest road out

Every spare dollar goes to the highest interest rate first. That single rule is the mathematically fastest and cheapest way to clear a set of debts. Put your real numbers in the ledger below and the whole plan recalculates.

Step seven

Levers that beat the spreadsheet

Cut the rate, not just the balance

A 0% balance-transfer card or a credit-union consolidation loan can drop a 25% APR to single digits. That does more in one afternoon than a year of extra payments. Count the transfer fee — usually 3–5% — and make sure you can clear the balance before the promo rate expires.

Keep one month of expenses in cash

Throwing literally every dollar at debt feels fastest, but a blown tyre puts it straight back on the card at 25%. A small buffer is not a detour — it's what keeps the plan from resetting.

Take the free money first

If your employer matches retirement contributions, that match beats almost any interest rate you're paying. Capture it, then aim everything else at the debt.

Freeze the balances

The schedule above assumes nothing new gets charged. One month of normal card spending can undo a quarter of progress. Move day-to-day spending to cash or debit until the cards are at zero.

Automate the payment, not the willpower

Set the full budgeted amount to leave your account the day after payday. Whatever is left is what you have to live on — decisions made once beat decisions made monthly.

When to break the rule

Avalanche is the cheapest order. If two rates are within a point or two of each other, clearing the smaller balance first costs you very little and gives you a win you can feel — and a plan you actually stick to beats an optimal one you abandon.

This is a calculator, not personalised financial advice. Figures are estimates: it assumes fixed APRs, fixed minimum payments, payments applied at month end after interest accrues, and no new borrowing.
